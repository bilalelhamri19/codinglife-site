2. Egoless Programming & Empathetic Code Reviews

You are not your code. Great engineers separate their personal self-worth from the code they produce. When someone critiques your pull request, they are refining the software, not attacking your intelligence. Treat review feedback with curiosity rather than defensiveness.

Golden Rule for Reviewers: Frame critiques as open questions: "Have you considered how this loop handles 10,000 items?" rather than authoritative commands: "This loop is bad, change it."

3. Ownership, Estimation & Managing Expectations

Junior developers often promise optimistic deadlines out of enthusiasm, leading to burnout and missed delivery targets. Senior developers deconstruct large ambiguous tasks into small verifiable milestones and communicate early whenever unexpected blockers arise.

The Rule: Under-Promise & Over-Deliver: Add a 20-30% buffer for testing, edge cases, and code reviews. If a task hits an unforeseen roadblock, inform your team immediately with potential alternatives.

4. Critical Thinking & First-Principles Problem Solving

Anyone can copy code from StackOverflow or prompt an AI model. What sets great software engineers apart is the ability to break an unfamiliar, messy real-world problem down to its fundamental first principles before writing a single line of syntax.

The Rubber Duck Technique: Explain the problem out loud to an inanimate object (or a colleague). In 80% of debugging dilemmas, the act of articulating the problem in plain human speech reveals the logical flaw.
