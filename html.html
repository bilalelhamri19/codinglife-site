1. The Semantic Revolution

In the professional world, <div> soup is a sign of an amateur. Semantic HTML is about using tags that describe their content, not their appearance.

Bad Practice <div class="header">...</div>
Pro Practice <header>...</header>

2. Meta Tags & SEO Fundamentals

Meta tags are the bridge between your site and search engines. Without proper meta tags, your content is invisible to Google.

<!-- Essential SEO -->
<meta name="description" content="Master HTML5 with our expert guide.">
<meta name="robots" content="index, follow">

<!-- Open Graph for Social Media -->
<meta property="og:title" content="HTML5 Expert Path">
<meta property="og:image" content="cover.jpg">

3. Document Structure Best Practices

A well-structured document follows a logical hierarchy. Use <main> for the primary content and <aside> for sidebars.

  • <section>: Groups related content together.
  • <article>: Independent, reusable content.
  • <nav>: Container for navigation links.

4. Forms & Advanced Validation

HTML5 introduced native validation that works without JavaScript. Use required, pattern, and type="email".

<input type="tel" pattern="[0-9]{10}" required placeholder="Phone Number">

5. Responsive Images with <picture>

Don't serve 4K images to mobile users. Use the <picture> tag to serve different resolutions based on screen size.

<picture>
  <source srcset="large.jpg" media="(min-width: 800px)">
  <img src="small.jpg" alt="Responsive Image">
</picture>

6. The Evolution of HTML

HTML has come a long way since 1991. From HTML 2.0 to the "Living Standard" of HTML5, each version added critical capabilities like multimedia and better structure.

7. ARIA Roles and Accessibility

8. Tabular Data vs Layout
